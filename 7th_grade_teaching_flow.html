About This Guide

This document presents a recommended instructional sequence for 7th Grade Florida BEST Mathematics Standards. Standards are organized by quarter and ordered to maximize conceptual coherence — foundational skills are introduced first, with applications and connections built on top as the year progresses.

How to Use

  • Numbered cards show the recommended teaching order within each quarter — start at 1 and work down.
  • Color-coded strand pills (NSO, AR, GR, DP) identify the domain of each standard.
  • Quarter 4 is a spiral & enrichment quarter — clusters build on all prior learning and can flex to meet student needs.
  • Cross-quarter notes call out where earlier skills unlock later content.
📌
Important — Recommendation, Not Mandate: The instructional sequence shown here is a recommendation based on conceptual coherence and pacing research. Teachers and curriculum coaches should adapt the order to fit their students’ needs, their school calendar, and district pacing guides. Professional judgment always comes first.

Recommended Teaching Sequence — 7th Standard

Standards within each quarter are ordered to maximize conceptual coherence — foundational skills first, culminating applications last. Each card explains why that standard belongs at that position. Cross-quarter notes highlight where Q1 skills unlock Q2 or Q3 content, and where Q3 content primes 8th grade readiness.

NSO — Number Sense & Operations
DP — Data Analysis & Probability
GR — Geometric Reasoning
AR — Algebraic Reasoning
QUARTER 1
Number Sense → Probability
1
NSO
MA.7.NSO.1.2
Multiply & Divide Rational Numbers
Opens the year by establishing operations with negative numbers. This is the computational engine that powers every probability calculation to follow.
↓
2
NSO
MA.7.NSO.2.2
All 4 Operations — Procedural Fluency
Consolidates NSO.1.2 with prior knowledge of addition and subtraction into a single fluency standard before students apply it in context.
↓
3
NSO
MA.7.NSO.2.3
Real-World Problems with Rational Numbers
Transfers fluency into multi-step real-world contexts. Closes the NSO thread and naturally pivots into probability data work.
↓
4
DP
MA.7.DP.2.1
Sample Space for Simple Experiments
Introduces probability by organizing and counting outcomes — no computation yet. Builds the conceptual foundation before numerical probability is introduced.
↓
5
DP
MA.7.DP.2.2
Interpret & Compare Probabilities
Places probability on a 0–1 number line and attaches likelihood language. Students use rational number comparison skills from NSO here.
↓
6
DP
MA.7.DP.2.3
Theoretical Probability of Simple Events
P = favorable ÷ total — directly applies the rational number division from step 1. Students also find complement probabilities, reinforcing the 0–1 range.
↓
7
DP
MA.7.DP.2.4
Experimental Probability & Simulations
Culminating Q1 standard. Compares experimental results to theoretical benchmarks and explores how larger samples produce more reliable estimates.
🔗 Unlocks in Q3: The rational number fluency built in steps 1–3 (NSO.1.2, 2.2, 2.3) is the prerequisite for Q3's NSO.1.1 and multi-step problem solving. Students revisit and complete their rational number understanding.
QUARTER 2
Geometry → Data → Percent
1
GR
MA.7.GR.1.1
Angle Relationships
Opens Q2 geometry by reinforcing equation-writing in a visual context. Complementary, supplementary, vertical, and adjacent angles are accessible entry points for algebraic thinking.
↓
2
GR
MA.7.GR.1.3
Triangle Angle Sum
Immediately extends GR.1.1 to triangles — students apply the same equation-writing skill with the new 180° constraint. A natural continuation.
↓
3
GR
MA.7.GR.1.4
Triangle Inequality & Construction
Adds side-length constraints to triangle thinking. Students explore when a valid triangle is possible — deepens geometric reasoning beyond angles alone.
↓
4
GR
MA.7.GR.1.5
Polygon Angle Sums
Culminates the geometry strand by generalizing the triangle angle sum to all polygons using (n−2)×180°. Students see how triangles decompose any polygon.
↓
5
GR
MA.7.GR.1.2
Scale Drawings
Shifts from angles to proportional geometry. Taught after the angle sequence because it calls for ratio reasoning — a natural bridge toward Q3 proportional relationships.
↓
6
DP
MA.7.DP.1.3
Statistical Displays & Distributions
Shifts the focus to data analysis. Can run alongside the end of the geometry sequence. Builds on Q1 data skills and prepares students for Q3 measure selection.
↓
7
AR
MA.7.AR.3.3
Percent Change & Multi-Step Financial Problems
Culminates Q2 by connecting Q1 rational number fluency to percent reasoning. A deliberate bridge: percent change is proportional thinking in disguise, setting up Q3's AR strand.
🔗 Unlocks in Q3: Scale drawings (GR.1.2) and percent change (AR.3.3) directly prime Q3 work on proportional relationships (AR.1.1, AR.1.2) and percent problems (AR.3.1). The transition is intentional.
QUARTER 3
Number Sense → Proportional Reasoning
1
NSO
MA.7.NSO.1.1
Add & Subtract Rational Numbers
Completes the rational number operations cycle. Students now hold all four operations with rational numbers — the algebraic fluency prerequisite for everything in Q3.
↓
2
NSO
MA.7.NSO.2.1
Solve Problems Using All Rational Operations
Multi-step problems that combine all four operations with all rational number forms. Directly bridges number sense fluency into algebraic problem solving.
↓
3
AR
MA.7.AR.1.1
Identify Proportional Relationships
Foundational algebraic reasoning standard for the quarter. Students recognize proportional patterns in tables, graphs, and equations — connects ratio understanding to algebra.
↓
4
AR
MA.7.AR.1.2
Constant of Proportionality
Deepens AR.1.1 by extracting and interpreting the unit rate k across all representations. Directly seeds 8th grade slope and linear function concepts.
↓
5
AR
MA.7.AR.3.1
Percent Problems
Applies proportional reasoning to tax, tip, discount, and markup. Connects AR.1.x to real-world financial contexts and reinforces Q2's percent change work.
↓
6
DP
MA.7.DP.1.4
Select Appropriate Measures & Interpret Data
Synthesizes all data work across Q1–Q3. Students justify which measure of center best represents a data set and analyze the effect of outliers — a high-level statistical thinking skill.
↓
7
AR
MA.7.AR.4.5
Proportional vs. Non-Proportional Relationships
Culminating standard of the year. Integrates proportional reasoning, multiple representations, and rate of change into one comprehensive analysis skill — the capstone of 7th grade math.
🚀 Prepares for 8th Grade: The constant of proportionality (AR.1.2) becomes slope. Proportional vs. non-proportional analysis (AR.4.5) becomes linear vs. nonlinear functions. Students who master Q3 enter 8th grade ready for MA.8.AR.
★ QUARTER 4

TEST PREP & FAST PM3 REVIEW

All 21 SCPS standards reviewed in five strategic clusters  ·  Spiraled practice toward mastery

Quarter 4 is not new content — it is a strategic, spiraled review of all Q1, Q2, and Q3 standards in preparation for the FAST PM3 assessment. Standards are grouped into five review clusters by conceptual strand, ordered to re-activate prior learning and connect skills across the year. Each cluster includes a focus priority and a common error alert for targeted re-teaching.

CLUSTER 1 — NUMBER SENSE
Weeks 1–2
NSO.1.2Multiply & Divide Rationals
NSO.1.1Add & Subtract Rationals
NSO.2.2Procedural Fluency
NSO.2.3Real-World Problems
NSO.2.1Multi-Step Rational Problems
Focus: All four operations with integers, fractions, and decimals — including negative values. Fluency with sign rules.
⚠ Common error: Sign mistakes when multiplying/dividing two negatives, and subtracting a negative (–(–x) = +x).
CLUSTER 2 — PROBABILITY
Week 3
DP.2.1Sample Space
DP.2.2Interpret & Compare Probabilities
DP.2.3Theoretical Probability
DP.2.4Experimental Probability
Focus: Connecting sample space to P = favorable/total; interpreting simulations; comparing experimental to theoretical probability.
⚠ Common error: Forgetting P(complement) = 1 – P(event); confusing "more trials" with "guaranteed accuracy."
CLUSTER 3 — GEOMETRY
Week 4
GR.1.1Angle Relationships
GR.1.3Triangle Angle Sum
GR.1.4Triangle Inequality
GR.1.5Polygon Angle Sums
GR.1.2Scale Drawings
Focus: Equation-writing for angle problems; applying (n−2)×180° for polygons; setting up proportions for scale drawings.
⚠ Common error: Applying the triangle angle sum to quadrilaterals; using the wrong formula for exterior angles.
CLUSTER 4 — DATA ANALYSIS
Week 5
DP.1.3Statistical Displays
DP.1.4Select Appropriate Measures
Focus: Choosing between mean, median, and mode for skewed data or outlier-heavy sets; justifying display selection (histogram vs. box plot vs. dot plot).
⚠ Common error: Using the mean when an outlier is present (median is more appropriate); confusing range with IQR.
CLUSTER 5 — PROPORTIONAL REASONING
Weeks 6–7
AR.1.1Proportional Relationships
AR.1.2Constant of Proportionality
AR.3.1Percent Problems
AR.3.3Percent Change
AR.4.5Proportional vs. Non-Proportional
Focus: Identifying k across representations; solving multi-step percent and financial problems; comparing proportional and non-proportional relationships.
⚠ Common error: Confusing percent of a number with percent change; assuming every linear relationship is proportional.
🎯 FAST PM3 Strategy: The PM3 assessment is cumulative — it tests all 21 standards from Q1, Q2, and Q3. Use Cluster 1 first because rational number fluency (NSO) is embedded in every other cluster. End with Cluster 5 (Proportional Reasoning) because AR standards carry the highest weight and connect most directly to 8th grade readiness. Interleave mixed-review practice sets between clusters to build retrieval strength across strands.
